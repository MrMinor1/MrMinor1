The best client conversation we have is telling someone not to hire us.

It happens more than you'd think. A W-2 earner with a standard deduction asks what we'd charge, and the honest answer is: nothing, because you shouldn't pay anyone. H&R Block's free tier covers roughly half of all filers; TurboTax's covers about a third. For a simple year, DIY software is genuinely the right tool, and saying so costs us nothing but earns something harder to buy — trust.

The interesting question is where the line actually sits. It isn't income level, and it isn't even self-employment — the $85–$119 self-employed tiers handle a clean 1099 year fine. The line is whether there are decisions in your year:

• Should this LLC elect S-Corp status at the current profit level?
• How should owner compensation be structured once payroll exists?
• Does the second state create nexus, and what does that cost?
• Should the equipment purchase land this year or next, and under which depreciation election?

Software is a reporting instrument. It records history accurately and files it on time. It will never tell you that a decision you're about to make has a five-figure tax consequence — because it only meets your year after the year is over.

So the useful framing for anyone asking "software or pro?" isn't complexity. It's: are you reporting a year, or shaping one?

Which side of that line was your last tax season on?
