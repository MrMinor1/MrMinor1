The largest small-business tax break in the code is also the least managed. Most owners couldn't tell you their number.

The QBI deduction — up to 20% of pass-through profit, off the top, no spending required — became permanent under last year's tax law. For 2026 it comes with wider phase-in ranges and a new $400 minimum for anyone with even $1,000 of active business income. For a business netting $150K, it's worth roughly $30K of deduction, every year, indefinitely.

Permanence changes the strategy conversation more than people realize. When QBI had an expiration date, optimizing around it felt like building on sand. Now it's load-bearing infrastructure, and three decisions quietly determine its size:

1. Owner compensation. S-Corp salary reduces QBI dollar for dollar. The salary that minimizes payroll tax and the salary that maximizes QBI are different numbers — the right answer is an optimization, not a rule of thumb.

2. Entity choice. The S-Corp election that saves self-employment tax can cost QBI. Above the thresholds (~$200K single / ~$400K joint in 2026), wages paid can INCREASE the deduction. The math flips by income level.

3. Retirement contributions. Pre-tax contributions lower taxable income — and can lower QBI too. Sometimes the Roth option wins for reasons nobody expects.

The pattern: QBI isn't claimed, it's shaped — by decisions made during the year, most of them before December.
