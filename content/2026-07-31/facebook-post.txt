There's a rule in the tax code that lets your business rent your home for up to 14 days a year — and you don't report a single dollar of that rent as income. 🏠

It's Section 280A(g). The IRS states it plainly: if you use a dwelling as a residence and rent it for fewer than 15 days during the year, you don't report the rental income and you don't deduct rental expenses.

Fewer than 15 means 14 days or fewer. Rent it on day 15 and the entire year becomes taxable.

Why owners care: it can work on both sides. Your business deducts a legitimate, reasonable rent as an ordinary expense. You exclude that same rent from income. Deducted once, taxed never.

Now the part the internet skips. Four conditions:

1️⃣ A real business purpose — an actual meeting, not a paper one.
2️⃣ A fair market rate, backed by written quotes from comparable venues.
3️⃣ Documentation — rental agreement, agenda, minutes, the actual payment.
4️⃣ Fourteen days. Count them.

And the honest limitation: if you're a sole proprietor or single-member LLC, this does nothing for you. With no separate entity you'd be paying rent to yourself — same pocket, nothing to exclude. It needs a real payer: an S-Corp, C-Corp or partnership.

Done sloppily, this is exactly the kind of deduction that unravels in an audit. Done properly, it's just the code working as written.
