Two retirement plans. Same self-employed income. One lets you shelter about $24,500 more than the other — and most owners pick the smaller one because it's easier to open. 🏦

Here's the 2026 math.

The employee deferral limit is $24,500 ($32,500 if you're 50+, and ages 60–63 get a larger catch-up of $11,250). The combined employee-plus-employer cap is $72,000.

Now the part that decides it: a SEP-IRA has ONE lever — an employer contribution based on a percentage of your earnings. A Solo 401(k) has TWO — that same employer percentage, PLUS your own salary deferral.

For a sole proprietor netting $100,000, roughly:
📉 SEP-IRA — about $18,600
📈 Solo 401(k) — about $43,100 ($24,500 deferral + the same ~$18,600 employer piece)

Same income. Same year. About $24,500 more sheltered, purely from plan choice.

And the timing, because this is the part people get wrong: the plan itself can now be ESTABLISHED as late as your return's due date including extensions. That's forgiving. But for a sole proprietor, the deferral election generally needs to be in place by December 31 — and the deferral is the larger half.

Which is exactly why this is an August conversation and not a December one.
