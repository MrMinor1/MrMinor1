"Which payroll software is cheapest?" is a question with a misleading answer, and the 941 deadline that just passed is a good time to explain why.

Published list pricing, August 2026, for three that small businesses actually shortlist:

• Patriot — $17/month Basic or $37/month Full Service, plus $4 per employee
• QuickBooks Payroll — $45/month, plus $5 per employee
• Gusto — $49/month, plus $6 per employee

On price alone Patriot wins by a distance. But the Basic tier calculates payroll; it doesn't file. The 941, the state returns and the W-2s remain yours. Full Service is the tier that files on your behalf.

So the real comparison at five employees isn't $37 against $79. It's $57 against $79 — once you're comparing products that do the same job. And if you choose Basic while believing you bought Full Service, you haven't saved $20 a month. You've quietly accepted a filing calendar, and penalties accrue against you rather than a vendor.

Which is the actual decision framework:

• Already running QuickBooks Online? QuickBooks Payroll almost always wins on integration alone. The reconciliation time saved exceeds the price difference.
• Growing, hiring, adding benefits? Gusto includes more of what you'll need next, and switching payroll mid-growth is genuinely painful.
• One or two employees, stable, and you will actually do the filings? Patriot Basic is a legitimate choice, not a compromise.

The framing I'd offer: payroll software isn't priced by features. It's priced by who carries the penalty when something is filed late.
