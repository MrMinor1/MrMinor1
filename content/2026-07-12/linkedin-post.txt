Most business owners have a "tax person." Far fewer have a tax strategy. The difference shows up in one question: when did you last talk about taxes BEFORE the year ended?

We run free 30-minute mid-year reviews, and the structure is deliberately boring — four checks, no pitch:

1. Effective rate vs. plan. First-half actuals against what the estimated payments assumed. This one check prevents most April surprises and most underpayment penalties.

2. Entity fit at current run rate. Owner compensation and entity structure are moving targets. Profit up 40% since the S-Corp math was last run? It's stale.

3. Deduction inventory. Not exotic loopholes — the ordinary ones left unclaimed: retirement plans not yet opened, accountable plans not set up, asset timing about to be fumbled.

4. Payroll and new-law exposure. Q2's Form 941 is due July 31. And with the new tips and overtime deductions, payroll systems that don't separately track qualified amounts are quietly creating January problems for employees and employers alike.

The pattern we see: businesses that do one structured mid-year conversation make better Q4 decisions with less stress, and file faster. Not because the review is magic — because someone finally looked before the game ended.
