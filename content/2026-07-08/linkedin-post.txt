Most business owners treat tax planning as an April event. By then, it's not planning — it's reporting.

Early July marks the midpoint of your tax year, and it's the single best time to change your outcome. Here's the mid-year framework we walk clients through:

1. Reforecast income. Compare H1 actuals to what your estimated payments assumed. If you're ahead of plan, adjust the Q3 payment (due September 15) — underpayment penalties are an avoidable interest expense.

2. Revisit owner compensation and entity structure. For an owner clearing six figures of profit, an S-Corp structure can shift a meaningful share of income out of self-employment tax — and a mid-year decision gives that math six months to work instead of six weeks.

3. Fund retirement with pre-tax dollars. The 2026 limits are meaningful: up to $72,000 across a Solo 401(k) or SEP-IRA. Cash-flowing it monthly from July is far easier than scrambling in April.

4. Fix your books before the mess compounds. Clean H1 data is what makes moves 1–3 possible. Whether it's software (QuickBooks Online, Xero) or outsourced help, the ROI isn't the bookkeeping itself — it's the decisions it unlocks.

The pattern behind all four: tax outcomes are determined by decisions made during the year, not by forms filed after it ends.
