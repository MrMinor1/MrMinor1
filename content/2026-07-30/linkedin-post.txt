The most valuable six weeks on a business owner's tax calendar start on Saturday, and almost nobody uses them.

Tomorrow, July 31, two filings are due: Form 941 for Q2, and Form 5500 or 5500-EZ for calendar-year 2025 benefit plans. The 5500 is the one that surprises people — plenty of solo 401(k) owners don't realise it applies to them until it's late.

One piece of fine print worth knowing: if you deposited all of your Q2 taxes on time, the 941 filing deadline moves to August 10. Miss a deposit and it doesn't. The extension rewards the depositing, not the filing.

After that, the calendar is empty until Q3 estimated tax on September 15.

That gap is the real opportunity, and the reason is arithmetic rather than motivation. A change made in early August compounds for five months. The identical change made in mid-December compounds for three weeks — and most of the meaningful levers have already closed by then. S-Corp elections, reasonable-compensation adjustments, retirement plan funding, equipment timing: every one of them needs runway.

There's a pattern I've watched for years. The owners who feel calm in April are not the ones who worked hardest in April. They're the ones who did one unhurried hour of work in a month when nothing was on fire.

Nobody is too busy in August. They're too busy in December — and by December, "too busy" has become expensive.
