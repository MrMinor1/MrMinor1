The most valuable tax rule for business owners isn't a loophole. It's published, permanent, and almost nobody uses it deliberately.

The safe harbor rule, in one paragraph: pay in — through withholding or quarterly estimates — either 100% of last year's total tax (110% if AGI exceeded $150,000) or 90% of the current year's tax, and the IRS cannot charge you an underpayment penalty. Full stop.

What makes this strategic rather than administrative:

1. It converts an unpredictable problem into a fixed number. You cannot know mid-year what you'll earn by December. You know exactly what last year's tax was. Safe harbor lets you anchor to the known number and stop guessing.

2. It's a legal float on your growth. Big year in progress? Pay 110% of last year's (smaller) tax, penalty-free, and the incremental tax on this year's growth isn't due until April. At ~6% penalty rates, that protection has real value — provided you actually reserve the difference instead of spending it.

3. The 90% option is the underused half. When revenue drops, owners routinely keep paying prior-year-sized estimates out of habit — an interest-free overpayment nobody notices until the refund. Down years should switch codes.

The failure mode isn't picking the wrong option. It's never picking at all — letting whatever your payroll or last year's vouchers happen to remit become your de facto strategy.
