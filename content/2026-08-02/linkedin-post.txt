A ranking exercise I run with business owners, because the answer is almost always backwards from what they expect.

Same business, $12,000 of tax owed. Four scenarios. Order them by cost:

• Pays three months late
• Files three months late
• Never tracked 8,000 business miles
• Spends $10,000 on December equipment "for the write-off"

The actual order, cheapest first:

Paying three months late costs about $180. The failure-to-pay penalty is 0.5% per month, plus interest.

Filing three months late costs about $1,800. Failure-to-file is 5% per month, capped at 25%. Identical delay, identical balance, ten times the penalty.

That ratio deserves to be better known. When cash is short, owners often delay the return because they can't fund the payment — which is precisely inverted. Filing without paying is roughly a tenth as expensive as paying without filing. If you do one thing, file.

Untracked mileage costs about $2,150. Eight thousand business miles at the 2026 standard rate of 72.5 cents is $5,800 of deduction that simply never existed on the return.

And the December equipment purchase costs about $6,300 — $10,000 of cash out to avoid roughly $3,700 of tax. A deduction discounts a purchase. It has never paid for one.

The pattern is what interests me. The two events that look like tax problems are the cheapest on the list. The two that look like diligence or shrewdness are the expensive ones, and neither generates a notice, so neither gets corrected.

Penalties announce themselves. Habits don't.

Which of the four has cost you the most — honestly?
