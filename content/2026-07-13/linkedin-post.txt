The home office deduction is a case study in how fear beats math.

Every year we meet business owners leaving $1,000–$3,000 on the table because someone at a barbecue told them the home office deduction "triggers audits." Meanwhile, the IRS publishes a simplified method specifically designed to make claiming it easy: $5 per square foot, up to 300 square feet, no receipts.

What the fear costs, in practice:

• A consultant with a 200 sq ft dedicated office skips the deduction for five years. That's $5,000 of simplified-method deductions forgone — more if actual expenses would have won.

• A contractor assumes he can't qualify because he works at job sites. But home is where the quoting, invoicing, and scheduling happen — which can make it his principal place of business under the IRS's own rules.

• An S-Corp owner doesn't realize the home office question changes shape with an entity — an accountable plan reimbursement, not a Schedule C deduction — and claims nothing at all.

The pattern: the deduction isn't complicated. It's the *decision framework* nobody runs — which test applies, which method wins this year, and how the answer changes with your entity structure.

Documentation is the entire game: measure the space, photograph it, keep the bills. A legitimate, documented deduction is boring — exactly how tax positions should be.
