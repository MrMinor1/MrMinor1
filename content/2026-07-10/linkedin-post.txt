Your employees are getting their tax advice from TikTok. That's now a payroll problem.

Since "no tax on tips" and "no tax on overtime" became law, we've heard some version of this from hourly workers everywhere: "Why is tax still coming out of my overtime check?"

Here's what owners of tipped and hourly teams need to be ready to explain in 2026:

1. These are deductions, not exemptions. Workers claim them on the new Schedule 1-A at filing time — up to $25,000 for qualified tips and $12,500 ($25,000 joint) for the overtime premium. Withholding on the paycheck doesn't simply stop.

2. Only the premium counts. For overtime, it's the "half" in time-and-a-half — not the whole check. Employees who expect the full amount tax-free will be disappointed, and they'll bring that disappointment to you.

3. Your reporting is the linchpin. The deductions depend on properly reported amounts on W-2s and 1099s. Sloppy payroll classification now means angry employees and amended forms later.

4. Income limits apply. Phaseouts start at $150,000 MAGI ($300,000 joint) — relevant for your higher earners.

The businesses that get ahead of this — a one-page explainer at onboarding, a payroll system that tracks qualified overtime separately — will spend zero hours in March arguing about paychecks.
